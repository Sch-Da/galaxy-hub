# Building a community - brick by brick

## Community building in the Digital Humanities Special Interest Group
What defines a community and how do you create a new one?
I have asked myself this question a lot since I started out at the Freiburg Galaxy Team.
Due to my background, it seemed only natural for me to start the Digital Humanities and Social Sciences special interest group.
But how does one set up a new community from scratch?  
I started from my own expertise in Digital Humanities, created workflows and tutorials so others could get an idea of Galaxy's capabilities.
 But the field is vast, and it was clear to me from the start that I would need lots of input from researchers from different fields to make Galaxy truly useful for a wide range of DH researchers.
I would need to transport my vision of DH's galactic journey much more than the status quo, if I wanted to build a community.

One of the people who saw this vision early on was [Jessica Wallace](). 
She is a researcher at the Department of Anglistics at the University of Freiburg, involved in the University's [Digital Humanities Lab]() and the [NFDI consortium Text+](). 
We had met several times - mostly around meetings of the university's [Central Data Facility (CDF)] when I pitched her my vision. 
She accepted the challenge to create one of her usual linguistics workflows on Galaxy and
suggested adding two command-line tools: [Montreal Forced Aligner ()]() and [new Fave]()

## Adding new linguistics tools: Montreal Forced Aligner and New Fave

Both are command-line tools, meaning researchers need a certain programming knowledge to use them.
This can be a barrier. Wrapping those tools by bringing them to Galaxy meant making them accessible through a command-line interface. That way, researchers without programming skills can also use them.
My colleagues [Armin Dadras]() and [Wolfgang Maier]() wrapped the tools and made them available as test tools.
Jessica tested a lot to find where the tools could need improvements and what tweaks would make them even more useful.
For this, domain knowledge is really of the essence, so Jessica's insights and enthusiasm were invaluable here.
